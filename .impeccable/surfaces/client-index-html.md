# Surface: The Dial (single-page app)

Scope: the entire v1 product — one page, the rig itself. Visitor mode: experience (the visitor is inside the instrument; success = tuning feels physical and a contact feels earned).

Audience & job: fun-community visitors landing with zero context; job is spin the dial, find a signal, make a QSO. Secondary audience: real hams judging fidelity of terminology (CQ, RST, QSB, 73, grid squares).

Action/task: flip POWER (this is the audio-unlock gesture), tune the VFO, read the traffic window, key a transmission, call CQ.

Proof/content: live NOAA K-index gates propagation (honest LIVE/EST source lamp); messages garble per-path server-side; everything ephemeral (~60s), nothing stored.

Chosen direction: "The Amber Shack Rig" — 1970s Japanese ham rig world (Kenwood TS-520 / Drake TR-4): brushed charcoal aluminum faceplate filling the viewport, giant round VFO knob with a backlit amber dial scale, analog S-meter, warm backlit traffic window, cream silkscreen labels, walnut end-cheeks. Seed key dial5eed, kind: pick, code-led.
Carried disciplines (from the direction round's raises, compatible with this world): one light source (glow only from functional lamps), visible construction (screws/seams/riveted data plate), honest signal states (measured vs estimated vs unreadable), instrument-grade panel density.

Memorable moment: flipping POWER — lamps warm up incandescently, band noise fades in, the rig comes alive; plus the weighted flywheel inertia of the VFO drag.

Unresolved: QSL cards + shack pages (v1.5); beacon bots, numbers station, day/night propagation (v2); deploy target (later, single small VM).
